title: Clips and the piano roll
about: Keep pieces of music as clips to put in again anywhere, and draw notes in the piano roll. About five minutes.

step
show: pattern
text: Clips are pieces of music kept with the song, to put in again wherever you like: a riff, a drum fill, a whole chorus.

  If the song has no notes yet, type a few first (space switches editing on, and the bottom row of letters plays the white keys).

step
show: pattern
text: Select some notes: drag over them with the mouse, or hold Shift and use the arrow keys. Select across several channels to keep them together.

step
show: menu Edit
text: Save the selection as a clip: Edit > Save selection as clip (or Ctrl+Shift+C), and give it a name.
wait: clip

step
show: clips
text: Your clip is in the clips panel. Its play button plays it, and right-clicking it shows what else it can do.

  Drag it into the pattern editor or the piano roll to put a copy in: you see where it'll go until you let go. Hold Ctrl as you let go for a linked copy: change one, and the others change with it.

step
show: button piano-roll
text: Now the piano roll: press its button (or View > Piano roll, Ctrl+Shift+P).
wait: command piano-roll

step
show: piano-roll
text: The piano roll shows the notes as bars: time from left to right, pitch from bottom to top, the keys on the left. The channel you're in is bright; the other channels' notes are faint behind it, and double-clicking one of them edits that channel.

  Click to put a note in (over a faint note too), drag a note to move it, or drag its end to make it longer; right-clicking a note erases it. Double-click a note for effects like vibrato or a slide. Ctrl+Z undoes any change.

step
show: window
text: Clips, the piano roll and the pattern editor all show the same notes, so use whichever feels easiest.

  If you'd rather work in the piano roll alone, untick View > Pattern editor: the piano roll takes its place, and the note keys type notes into it while editing is on.
