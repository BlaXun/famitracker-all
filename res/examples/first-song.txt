; Help > Example songs: the walkthrough of first-song.ftm, which opens with it.
; The format is in Source/TutorialScript.h.

title: First song
about: A tune, a bass and drums on the NES's own chip, and what an instrument's volume envelope does. About five minutes.

step
show: window
text: This is an example song, opened as a new song: change anything you like. When you save it, FamiTracker asks for a name, so the example itself stays as it is.

  The box in front holds the song's comments, a few lines on what it shows (Module > Comments shows them again). Close it, then press Next.

step
show: pattern
play: bars 1-16
text: First the whole song: 16 bars, about half a minute. Press Play below and watch the pattern editor follow the music down the rows. The walkthrough goes on when it's done.
wait: heard

step
show: channel Pulse 1
play: bars 1-4
solo: Pulse 1
text: The tune is on Pulse 1, the first column. Each row with a note is a moment a note starts: E-3 is the note E, and the 3 says how high (a higher number, higher notes). The 00 next to it is the instrument it plays with.

  This step lets only Pulse 1 be heard, so the other channels' names at the top turn grey. Listen to the tune alone.

step
show: channel Triangle
play: bars 1-4
solo: Triangle
text: The triangle plays the bass, the low notes under the tune: a few in every bar, now and then the same note an octave higher.

  The triangle can't play louder or softer: a note is on or off. That's why the bass sounds as strong as it does.

step
show: channel Noise
play: bars 1-4
solo: Noise
text: The drums are on the noise channel. A kick, a snare and a hi-hat are noise at different pitches, each with a short instrument of its own (03 to 07). The noise channel plays one drum at a time.

step
show: channel Pulse 1
play: bars 7-10
solo: Pulse 1
text: At bar 9 the tune starts again with instrument 01 instead of 00. The notes are the same. Listen across the change: up to bar 8 each note stays loud while it lasts; from bar 9 each one fades away by itself, like a plucked string.

step
show: instruments
instrument: 01
text: What makes the difference is the instrument's volume envelope. Instrument 01 is picked in the list: double-click it to open it.

  The bars of its Volume graph are its loudness a sixtieth of a second at a time: high at the start, then falling to nothing. Instrument 00's bars fall a little and then stay up. Close the editor when you've had a look.

step
show: channel Pulse 2
play: bars 9-12
solo: Pulse 1, Pulse 2
text: In bars 9 to 16, Pulse 2 joins in with a second voice a little under the tune, so two notes sound together. Here are the two pulses together.

step
show: frames
text: The frame list is the song's order. Each line plays one pattern for every channel, two bars here, and after the last line the song starts again from the top.

  The coloured bands beside it name the song's parts: "Held" for bars 1 to 8 and "Plucked" for bars 9 to 16.

step
show: window
text: That's the whole song. Now make it yours: switch editing on (space) and type new notes over the tune, or pick instrument 01 and play the keys. Ctrl+S saves your version under a name of your own.

  Help > Example songs has three more: NES tricks, the VRC6, and soft sounds in stereo.
